A useful website brief explains your customers, what they need and the action you want them to take.

## Start with the enquiry you want

Define the visitor who should contact you and what makes their enquiry useful. A software project may need a discussion about workflows; a cloud migration needs a view of the current infrastructure. Your website should help each visitor find the relevant service and share enough context to start a useful conversation.

## Give each distinct service its own page

List the services customers actually buy. For each one, prepare an explanation of the problem it solves, what is included, the expected deliverables and common questions. A visitor looking for a mobile app should be able to understand that service without reading a generic list of everything your company does.

## Gather evidence you can publish

Prepare approved project examples, relevant screenshots and customer feedback you have permission to use. Describe your role and the limits of any result. If you do not have publishable evidence yet, explain your process clearly and add real project evidence as it becomes available.

## Plan where enquiries go

A success message should mean a request was saved. Decide who receives notifications, who checks the database when email fails and who follows up. Ask for only the fields you need and tell visitors how their information is used.

## Include search and accessibility in the brief

Agree descriptive page titles, readable headings, canonical URLs, a sitemap and internal links. Plan for mobile use, visible form labels, keyboard access and meaningful error messages. Check that business details match your approved public profiles.

## Agree launch ownership

Document who controls the domain, hosting, source code, analytics and email configuration. Include redirects from old URLs, backups, an update plan and a final test of every enquiry route.

## A simple starting brief

Send your main business goal, target customers, required services, existing website address, content availability, integrations and preferred timeline. Your development partner can then help identify gaps and propose a realistic scope.
